### Palette application

This plain HTML portfolio uses a bespoke editorial color system rather than shadcn tokens. The page background is warm paper (`#FAF3E4`), deep ink text (`#261D17`), muted supporting copy (`#62564B`), print-red accents (`#A63F29`), and quiet plant greens for illustrated details (`#536648`). Artwork supplies additional colors. No dark theme is used; the light print-like gallery keeps the illustrations legible and front-and-center.

### Contrast

Primary interface colors use dark rust for smaller accent text and accessible ink on the warm paper. The deeper rust (`#96381F`) serves as a filled action with white text and exceeds WCAG AA (about 6:1); the bright seed accent is decorative and not used for small body copy. Muted copy is deliberately darker than a decorative pale tan to stay readable on paper.

## Typography — Fraunces + DM Sans

- **Display:** Fraunces. Use its expressive serif for portfolio headlines, story titles, and brief handwritten-feeling notes.
- **Body and controls:** DM Sans. Use for navigation, descriptions, captions, and interaction labels.

Both fonts are loaded from Google Fonts. Georgia and Helvetica/system sans-serif act as fallbacks.

## Tone and voice

### Words to use

Human, friendly, and encouraging. Describe the feeling, character, technique, or little story behind each drawing. Use concrete words and natural first-person language.

### Words to avoid

Corporate language, empty enthusiasm, and adjective piles. Avoid overclaiming experience or presenting fictional portfolio projects as commissioned client work.

### Voice example

> “A little world full of curious faces. Loose sketches first, then the color that brings each one to life.”

## Usage dos and don'ts

**Do:**

- Let the illustrations be the color; use the paper-and-ink palette to frame them.
- Use Fraunces for personality and DM Sans for clarity.
- Give each piece context: medium, purpose, and the choices behind its character design.
- Keep the sketching process visible and celebrate exploratory linework.
- Use visible focus states, reduced-motion support, and meaningful alt text.

**Don't:**

- Claim unsupplied client work, awards, role history, or credentials.
- Hide sketches behind finished illustrations; the role prioritizes process and drawing fundamentals.
- Use the bright accent rust for small paragraphs.
- Add gradients, glossy card treatments, or generic stock imagery that fight the handmade art direction.
- Forget to replace the portfolio name and contact placeholders before publishing.
